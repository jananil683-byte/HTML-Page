Hello

"Hello" is a universally recognized greeting and salutation used in the English language to acknowledge someone's presence, initiate a conversation, or answer a telephone call. First attested in writing in 1826, the word gained massive popularity in the late 19th century when Thomas Edison championed it as the standard telephone greeting, famously beating out Alexander Graham Bell's preferred choice, "Ahoy." Etymologically, it is linked to older words like hallo and the French exclamation holà, which were originally used to catch someone's attention or command a horse to stop. Today, "hello" serves as a foundational piece of social etiquette. It is versatile enough to be used in formal professional settings, yet warm enough to welcome a close friend.
